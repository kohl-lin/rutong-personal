## Observed Deck Traits

- Real Shopee internal decks are operationally dense: tables, status trackers, issue lists, metric logic, and workflow diagrams are common.
- Many pages combine a crisp title with dense supporting evidence below, not a single big visual.
- Orange is present but often restrained in business decks; gray, black, white, and table lines carry much of the information.
- Recurring decks rely heavily on agenda pages, section headers, page numbers, and meeting-date context.
- Actionability matters: pages often need PIC/owner, deadline, status, follow-up, risk, and next-step fields.
- Backup/appendix pages are common and should be supported rather than treated as an afterthought.
- Internal keynote template pages use a no-text Shopee mark, thin orange top rule, concise orange section labels, black business-answer titles, and light gray footer/page context.
- The internal template explicitly teaches document structure: executive summary, context and objectives, background facts and data, findings and discussion points, conclusion and next steps.
- Source decks with stronger visual analysis elements often rely on shape grammar rather than illustration: pyramids for hierarchy/composition, chevrons for process, quadrants for tradeoff, status dots for health, and rounded cards for KPI summaries. Reuse the logic, then restyle with Shopee tokens.

## Common Slide Patterns

Use these as layout requirements when finishing templates:

- Cover: deck title, date, meeting cadence, team, confidentiality marker when relevant.
- Agenda: numbered sections, often with 2-4 major blocks.
- Key highlights: 3-5 main messages, each with a short explanation or quantified proof.
- KPI dashboard: multiple metric cards, period comparisons, target markers, deltas, and status colors.
- Performance review: YTD/Y25/Y26 review, OKR target vs actual, trend, and commentary.
- Deep dive: one issue or metric with drivers, root cause, evidence, and implications.
- Timeline/phase plan: months/weeks, launch phases, check-ins, owner and milestone markers.
- Workflow/process: step-by-step flow, handoffs, dependencies, and escalation routes.
- Hierarchy/composition: segmented pyramid, tier stack, funnel mix, severity ladder.
- Comparison/decision: 2x2 quadrant, before/after bridge, option split, ranked bar list.
- Risk/challenge: issue, impact, root cause, mitigation, owner, and expected closure.
- Metric definition: reporting logic, calculation, scope, exclusions, and example interpretation.
- PIC/stakeholder matrix: function, responsibility, key PICs, dependency, support needed.
- Follow-up tracker: topic, follow-up, PIC, key update, status, next action.
- Appendix/backup: details, raw cuts, extra tables, and supporting evidence.
- Q&A/training: questions, answers, examples, non-examples, and knowledge checks.
- Communication template: What it is, why it is important, best practices, then sample. This is useful for training agents to make decks that explain their own logic.
